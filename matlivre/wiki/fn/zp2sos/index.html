zp2sos

Converts zeros, poles and gain into second-order sections.

Category: Filter conversions

Syntax

sos = zp2sos(z, p, k)

Section matrix with the gain absorbed in it.

[sos, g] = zp2sos(z, p, k)

Also returns the overall gain separately.

[sos, g] = zp2sos(z, p, k, order, scale)

Chooses 'up' or 'down' ordering and 'none', 'inf' or 'two' scaling.

Description

Poles are grouped in pairs (complex conjugates together, real poles two at a time) and each pair is matched with the nearest zeros, so every section is a real biquad. This gives a cascade that is far less sensitive to coefficient rounding than the direct form.

The number of zeros may not exceed the number of poles. Missing zeros are filled with zeros at the origin so each section has three numerator coefficients.

Input arguments

z
Zeros, as a vector. Complex zeros must come in conjugate pairs.
p
Poles, as a vector. Complex poles must come in conjugate pairs. There cannot be more zeros than poles.
k
Gain, a scalar.
order
'down' (default) puts the poles closest to the unit circle last; 'up' puts them first.
scale
'none' (default), 'inf' or 'two': scale the sections by the infinity norm or the 2-norm to reduce overflow.

Output arguments

sos
L-by-6 matrix, one section per row, [b0 b1 b2 a0 a1 a2], with a0 = 1.
g
Overall gain. With one output it is folded into the first section.

Example

Four poles and four zeros

z = [0.5; -0.5; 1; -1];
p = [0.2+0.3i; 0.2-0.3i; 0.6; 0.1];
[sos, g] = zp2sos(z, p, 2);
disp(round(sos*1e4)/1e4)
disp(g)
Output
    1.0000    1.5000    0.5000    1.0000   -0.4000    0.1300
    1.0000   -1.5000    0.5000    1.0000   -0.7000    0.0600
     2
